# Design quality standard

PPD should reject generic or careless output rather than treating successful generation as approval.

## 1. Art direction

- Every deck has an intentional visual identity appropriate to its subject and audience.
- Typography, colour, grid, imagery and visual motifs are selected as a coherent system.
- Reference material informs principles; it is not copied.

## 2. Anti-slop defaults

- No decorative icon beside every heading.
- No invented logos or meaningless brand marks.
- No default purple/blue gradients, floating blobs or random geometric decoration.
- No repeated card grids as the universal layout.
- No generic stock image chosen only because a slide has empty space.
- No AI-generated image by default; exceptions require an explicit brief-level decision.
- No unnecessary shadows, bevels, 3D effects or ornamental transitions.

## 3. Composition

- Every slide has a clear primary focal point and a purposeful reading order.
- Layout responds to the information type and slide objective.
- Whitespace is deliberate; density is appropriate to the delivery mode.
- Deck-wide consistency does not require identical layouts.

## 4. Authenticity and evidence

- Visual assets retain source, creator and licence/provenance metadata.
- Important claims and quantitative statements retain evidence references.
- Charts use accurate scales, units, labels and source notes.
- Images are relevant to the claim and are not presented as documentary evidence when synthetic or illustrative.

## 5. Editability

- Important text, charts, tables, labels, diagrams and connectors remain native/editable wherever practical.
- Flattening is reserved for complex imagery or compositions where raster/vector artwork is the correct medium.
- Export fidelity and editability are tested separately.

## 6. Hard rejection conditions

A slide must not pass when it contains critical overflow/clipping, missing fonts/assets, broken layout, unsupported key claims, materially inaccurate data, untraceable required assets or an export mismatch that changes meaning.

## 7. Human evaluation

Aesthetic judgement should not rely on one model-generated score. Use blind comparisons, task-specific rubrics and feedback from people who regularly create or review presentations. Track both perceived quality and the amount of manual correction required.
