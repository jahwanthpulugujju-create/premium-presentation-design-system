# Product vision

## Mission

Build a presentation design system that combines the reasoning of a strategist, the visual judgement of an art director and the precision of a presentation engineer.

## Problem

Many automated presentation tools optimise for speed and template completion. Their outputs can feel interchangeable: repeated card grids, decorative icons, generic gradients, irrelevant synthetic imagery and flattened diagrams. Users still spend substantial time correcting narrative, visual hierarchy, factual detail and PowerPoint editability.

## Product proposition

PPD turns briefs, research and data into visually distinctive, evidence-aware and editable presentations. It treats presentation creation as a multi-stage design process rather than a single text-to-slides operation.

## Target users

- Founders and product teams creating pitches and strategic presentations.
- Researchers and students communicating technical findings.
- Consultants and operators producing decision-oriented decks.
- Designers who want an accelerated but controllable production workflow.

## Product principles

1. Quality over first-pass speed.
2. Narrative before slide layout.
3. Art direction before decoration.
4. Authentic assets before synthetic filler.
5. Content-aware composition before fixed templates.
6. Editability as a first-class requirement.
7. Automated checks with human-reviewable evidence.
8. Honest status reporting: planned features are never represented as shipped.

## Initial scope

The first proof of concept is deliberately narrow: three slide concepts—an editorial opener, a data-storytelling slide and an editable technical architecture slide. A complete presentation generator is outside the first milestone.

## Success criteria

The MVP is successful when it can produce those three concepts with a consistent but non-repetitive visual identity, correct content, traceable visual assets, native editable PowerPoint elements and no critical rendering defects. Quality should be judged through blind human evaluation against comparable tools and a documented rubric.
